Property notes

A large medieval stone range seen from open ground below and to one side, comprising a two storey wing with mullioned and transomed leaded windows under a shallow hipped roof, a central gabled cross wing carrying a tall traceried window of five lights above a row of small dark openings at ground level, and a three storey square tower at right with square headed windows, one empty unglazed light near the top, and a broad battered spur of masonry rising diagonally across its flank. Walls are coursed rubble with dressed quoins, heavily weathered and colonised by lichen. The foreground is a level gravel apron, clear of vegetation, with a notice board on posts at the right. The sky is bright with cloud behind the roofs.

History of the incident

I have kept this gate nineteen years, and the guidebooks have one article of faith that they got from me: the mound was raised for the keep, and there is nothing under this castle but the hill, gravel to the clay. I hold the keys to every vault and passage there is. The equinoctial gales undo a little of the ground each year, and each year the ground gives a little back. A long bone on the apron I entered as ox, midden scatter. The garrison buried its dead of the Revolutionary War east of the kitchens; everyone said so, and I said so too, with the torch, to visitors. The low openings under the great window breathe warm in a hard frost; bakery flue, I entered. The bones came up laid, not scattered, in courses a spade could follow, and I read a robbed drain run, took up what the gale gave, and rebedded it in the border by the gate, in rows, where the digging is easy. The Ministry likes a tidy return.

L. M____, for the region, telephoned on the 20th of January: the file closes on the 30th, the castle with it, and the Meteorological Office had warned a gale for the night of the 26th, the worst in my nineteen years. I set the mason to the low openings and took the tower shutters myself, up the spur stair, before the light went. At the stair's foot the wind had already lifted the slab. I put the torch in. No gravel. No clay. Bone, laid in courses under the whole of the courtyard, and the foot of our stair corbelled on the top course like a stud set in it, and the courses running on under the keep and the gate, past anywhere I hold keys. My vaults had been mouths. I went the rounds at dusk with the ring, as I have done nineteen years, and shut them, and understood for the first time what the closing of them was for.

The gale blew itself out on the 27th. I filed the damage return: slates and boards, nothing of note. I said nothing of the courses. The border by the gate runs to the same lines as the courses below, because I dug it so, row under row, nineteen seasons. I was never keeping the castle. The top course is mine.
