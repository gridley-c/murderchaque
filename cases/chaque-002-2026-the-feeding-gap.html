Case file · do not circulate · CHAQUE/002/2026

The Feeding Gap

File no. CHAQUE/002/2026 · Recorded 18 October 2026 · Location: Brick barn on a cinder track, near Rocester, north of Uttoxeter, Staffordshire. · Status: OPEN

Written by: county constabulary records clerk, 1974

×

Exhibit A — retained in original condition. 35mm at f/2.0; captured 28 February 2026.

Property notes

Two storey brick barn under a collapsed roof, gable end facing the cinder track. Gable wall carries a loft door set in brickwork pierced by pigeon holes. Rafter stumps project from wall plates. Ground and first floor sash windows on the flank elevation are shuttered. One small arched opening pierces the flank wall low down, approximately two feet above ground level. An eight bar metal gate leans against the flank wall, bearing a weathered notice. Surrounding vegetation of bare scrub and willow. Monochrome negative.

History of the incident

In March 1881 the barn stood on the holding of T. M____, cowman, late of the parish. The arched opening low on the flank was cut at his instruction, to pass feed to his tethered bull without opening the doors. On the third night of that month the village heard the beast screaming. It did not stop. T. M____ entered the barn with lantern and hay knife. He did not come out. A neighbour found the lantern burning low and the bull dead of terror at its tether, its horns driven into the floorboards. Of T. M____ only his boots remained, set side by side near the feeding gap, laced tight.

The property passed to a cousin in Leicestershire who would not enter. A labourer was paid to board the doors. He reported the boots gone the next morning, though he had set the last plank himself and watched the sun rise on it. The pigeon holes were bricked over in 1893. The roof fell in 1914. The estate papers first mention an anomaly in 1962: a valuation officer notes the flank wall opening is not shown on the original plans, yet appears worn smooth on the interior face, as by long use. He does not say by what.

The file cannot account for the plans. The 1840 tithe survey shows the barn plain-walled. Every later survey includes the gap. The contractors who bricked the gable in 1893 left the low opening unrecorded in their bill. The bull was slaughtered and sold for meat. No death certificate exists for T. M____, no body being found, yet parish records carry his burial entry, dated a week later, plot unmarked. The boots have been recovered three times: 1881, 1893, and 1974. Each time they were identified by the lacing pattern. Each time they were placed in evidence stores. The pigeon holes stand open to the sky, though the bill of 1893 records them bricked over. The stores are empty. The gap remains.
