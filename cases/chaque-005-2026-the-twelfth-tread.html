The Twelfth Tread

File no. CHAQUE/005/2026 · Recorded 26 October 2026 · Location: Open ground at the fortified manor, Shropshire · Status: OPEN

Written by: Ministry of Works inspector, 1954

×

Exhibit A — retained in original condition. 24mm at f/8; captured 26 October 2026

Property notes

A medieval fortified manor keep of rubble stone standing on open ground and seen in monochrome. An external stair of solid masonry rises the right hand face of the keep to a dark doorway at first floor level set under a small gabled stone hood; the stair has a solid cheek wall and no handrail. Dressed stone parapets with squared merlons crown the wall head. Windows at ground level carry leaded diamond glazing. A gabled wing of two storeys with mullioned and transomed windows and a pitched tiled roof abuts the left flank of the keep at a straight vertical joint, with one chimney stack behind its ridge. A single modern interpretation panel on one post stands at the foot of the stair buttress. The foreground is level gravel with sparse growth at the wall foot. No persons are present.

History of the incident

On 14 October 1954 the keep was attended by me, H. R____, inspector for the Ministry of Works, to resurvey the stair before the amended plan was lodged. The building stood as the card describes: rubble stone on open ground, dressed parapets above, leaded diamond glazing at ground level, the Georgian wing set against the left flank. The external stair rises the right hand face in solid masonry, plain treads, no rail, to a dark doorway at first floor level under its little stone hood. I counted the treads for the measured drawing. Eleven. The survey of 1913 gives twelve.

I entered eleven and put the difference down to make-up work. The Office masons had relaid treads in the nineteen twenties, and a tread could have gone under the gravel spread at the foot. The file gave against me. The 1913 surveyor had counted twelve, twice, on two visits. Behind his figure stands older paper. In 1683 a servant of the house was found dead at the foot of the stair, fallen from the door above, her neck broken, and the coroner's note counts the steps she fell as twelve. I went back on 15 October and counted from the foot, rod to each tread. Twelve. On 16 October, at dusk, thirteen.

The keep is as I first wrote it. The stair is as I first wrote it. The number is not. Three counts, three pages, initialled, no two agreeing, and the form wants one figure in one box. I cannot close the count, and I will not certify what will not close. The building I certify. The stair I return unengrossed. The Ministry sends the form back with a clean margin, and I attend again, and count again, and each time the stair holds one tread more than the page before it. The keep stands as it stood. The wing and the parapets stand. The door at the head of the stair stands open above whatever count the stair is holding. The box is empty. The count will not sit still.
