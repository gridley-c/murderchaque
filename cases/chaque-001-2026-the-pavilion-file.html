The Pavilion File

File no. CHAQUE/001/2026 · Recorded 18 October 2026 · Location: Pavilion ground among the pines, Telford, Surrey · Status: OPEN

Entertained by: parish clerk and records officer, 2023

Exhibit A — retained in original condition. 22 January 2023 at 11:36; 19mm at f/2.0; 1/3000s; ISO 320; no GPS in the file; retained in original condition.

Property notes

Single storey timber pavilion under a pitched tiled roof. Central gable projects over an open verandah, clad in herringbone boarding with green painted battens. Gilt numerals 1883 fixed beneath a small square clock face centred in the gable. Twin sash windows flank a central doorway, both sashes showing multiple panes. Open verandah with bench seating runs the width of the front elevation. Dark stained timber outbuildings stand left and right of the frame. Ground to the fore is bare earth and gravel, wet, with standing water in wheel ruts. Tall pines crowd close behind the rear wall. Sky overcast, light flat.

History of the incident

The pavilion was unlocked on 22 January 2023 by the groundsman, E. V____, who found the door open and the clock stopped at 11:36. The gilt numerals 1883 beneath the dial were bright as new, though the last gilding in the minute books is dated 14 March 1937. A line of footprints crossed the dust from the door to the centre of the floor and back by the same steps. No other mark. The ground outside was wet from overnight rain, as the plate records, yet no prints entered from without. The honorary secretary, R. B____, wound the movement. It was sound in every part. It would not run. It runs now and keeps true time, but only while the door stands open. Locked, the hands return to 11:36 within the hour.

The matter passed to paper on 3 March 2023, when R. B____ asked the parish clerk, T. H____, to record the clock as defective. On 10 March the assistant archivist, M. D____, surveyed the building. She photographed the gable, remarked that the numerals seemed lately re-gilt, and took rubbings. Her report of 24 March finds the structure sound and the movement of a common pattern, with no sign of tampering. The council resolved on 5 April to take no further action. The minute book went back to the scorebox. On 19 April the scorebox was found forced. The minute book alone was gone. A new book opens on 21 April with an entry recording the loss.

The file does not close. The plate before me shows the pavilion on 22 January 2023: gilding bright, ground wet, pines crowding close, the small square clock face too distant to read. Yet the exposure was made at 11:36. The register in my office carries the entry of 3 March 2023 in the hand of T. H____, and beneath it, in the same hand, the words: "The clock was not stopped. It was showing the time of the photograph." The line is not initialled. T. H____ has no memory of writing it. No such note appears in the book that replaced the one that was lost. The ink is iron-gall and has browned as a century browns, though the entry is not three years old. The book is in the evidence locker. The register remains open at that page.
