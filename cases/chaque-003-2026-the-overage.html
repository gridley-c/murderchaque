The Overage

File no. CHAQUE/003/2026 · Recorded 18 October 2026 · Location: Church of St Cadoc, Llangattock Lingoed, Monmouthshire · Status: OPEN

Written by: diocesan records clerk, 1957

×

Exhibit A — retained in original condition. 23mm at f/2.0; captured 2 March 2024.

Property notes

Whitewashed rubble nave and chancel under stone slate roofs. Gabled south porch with limewash flaking to bare stone. Dark arched doorway behind the porch door. Churchyard crowded with leaning headstones, including one heavy marker leaning hard in the foreground, and ivy shrouded tombs. Yew boughs above. Wet grass. Flat March light.

History of the incident

In March 1891 the parish sold plot 114 in St Cadoc's churchyard to a widow of the parish for the burial of her son, drowned at Newport docks that winter. The sexton, S. P____, opened the ground on the fourth of the month. At five feet his spade struck wood. Not roots. Boards, sound under rot, and beneath them a coffin whole and occupied. No entry in the burial register answered to plot 114. No stone stood above it. The ground, by every paper the parish kept, was a virgin plot sold in good faith. S. P____ dug a second hour by lantern to be sure of what he had found. He was sure. He closed the cut and reported to the incumbent. The drowned son was buried two days later in plot 121, and no further word was written of the man under 114.

In the register the sale line was struck through with one stroke and initialled. The found coffin was left where it lay, the cut turfed. S. P____ kept his own tally of the yard in a pocket book he was never asked to show. In it he noted that the yard by his count had received forty-one burials in twenty years against thirty-seven entered in the parish register. He did not accuse. He counted. When he died in 1909 the new sexton found the book, read the arithmetic, and put it away. The book passed with the parish papers to the registry, and its arithmetic is set down here.

The problem is in the papers now. The register records every sale and every interment, and the two columns do not close. Ground sold once was sold twice. Ground with no sale on record came up occupied. A survey in 1952 found the yard surface heaved and set so out of level that eleven stones were leaning past repair, the heaviest of them hard over in the wet ground, and the surveyor wrote that the churchyard appeared fuller than any plan of it could explain. The paper cannot hold the number the yard holds. The register was last reconciled against the ground in 1957 and the count was left unfinished. It is unfinished still.
