Beneath the Boards

File no. CHAQUE/001/2026 · Recorded 18 October 2026 · Location: Pavilion ground among the pines, Telford, Surrey · Status: OPEN

Entertained by: county constabulary clerk, 1959

Exhibit A — retained in original condition. Captured 22 January 2023 at 11:36; 19mm at f/2.0; 1/3000s; ISO 320; no GPS in the file; retained in original condition.

Property notes

A single storey cricket pavilion of timber construction beneath a pitched roof, its walls painted white, fronted by a central verandah under a projecting gable. The gable carries herringbone boarding with green painted battens and bears beneath its apex a small square clock face over gilt numerals reading 1883. Twin sash windows flank a central doorway which stands dark and open; benches line the verandah. To the left stand weatherboard sheds and a wooden yard gate swung open; to the right a black boarded outbuilding with a single green painted door and a corrugated porch roof. A gravel approach path, wet and scored, runs the length of the view over bare earth, with frost lying on the grass margins and a white timber post on the right hand verge. Mature pines close behind the building on all sides under flat winter daylight.

History of the incident

The groundsman, W. H., was last seen alive on Friday, 16 January 1959, when he left this ground at dusk, his Friday round done and the gable clock wound for the week. He did not come home. The ground stood empty in the close season, and nine days passed before two constables on the winter rounds found the yard gate ajar and the central doorway of the pavilion standing open, as both stand in the plate. His dinner pail lay on the verandah bench. His coat was gone. Snow had fallen and melted again, and no track kept. Above the doorway the gable clock had stopped. Its hands stood at eleven thirty-six.

The dogs were brought on the second day, and they drew the verandah and would not leave it. On 27 January the boards were taken up. W. H. lay beneath them in the shallow black of the crawl space, no more than eighteen inches of room beneath the joists. He had been laid there, and the boards nailed down over him from above, and he was so nailed while he yet lived. The coroner recorded clay between his teeth and splinters of deal beneath the nails of both hands, and gave the opinion that he had lived some days in the dark under his own pavilion and had torn at the boards until his strength failed. Every nail driven through those boards was new and bright, and of a gauge the pavilion stores did not stock.

Here the record fails. W. H. wound the gable clock each Friday at noon, and the station return states that the movement was taken as an exhibit, stopped at eleven thirty-six, and booked into the store at Telford. The exhibit book holds the entry. The store does not hold the clock. No receipt exists for its return, and the survey photograph of 22 January 2023, exposed at eleven thirty-six in the morning, shows a small square clock face hanging beneath the numerals 1883 where the clock always hung. The boards of the verandah were renewed with pine of the stocked gauge. No person has been charged. The file cannot be closed, for the clock is in two places, and the doorway the return records standing open stands open in the plate still.
