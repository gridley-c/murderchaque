Property notes

Red brick mill of four storeys on flat wet ground near Rocester, Staffordshire. Coursed brick elevations with regular tiers of sash loom windows, small paned, some upper openings holding louvred ventilator panels. Slate roofs of shallow pitch, a brick stack above the ridge. A taller entrance tower breaks forward between the ranges in lighter brick with pilaster strips, round arched windows and a corbelled cornice, and a single dark door at its base carries a small pale notice fixed with nails. Security lamps are mounted on the walls. Bare poplars stand along the frontage. Rough wet ground fills the foreground under flat February light.

History of the incident

Fourteenth of January 1910. H. W____ worked the night at loom 19, top floor, under the loom windows, alone on that floor by then. He had kept that frame since it was set. At the changeover his sleeve went into the belt. Then his arm. Then the rest of him. Nothing stopped the loom. It wove on until the overlooker came up at six. What the bolt held was cut out and burned in the yard before the constable arrived. The mill doctor gave the cause as crush injuries. The coroner sat at Uttoxeter and returned misadventure. The widow drew eleven shillings. Loom 19 was wiped down and left idle.

In February the fitting shop set loom 19 back on. I keep the labour book and the register of looms. The register is copied from the counter on each frame, and the counter counts the shuttle. From the first week the two books would not agree. The register carried a Friday night shift for which no hand had been paid. Friday night had been his shift. I ruled it a copying error and carried the figure on. It came again the following Friday, and every Friday after. The night watchman left when he found the beam dressed and full and no lamp lit on that stair. In March I went up myself. The floor was dark. The loom was warm.

The cloth agreed with the register. I set the warehouse tally against the paid shifts, and the surplus stood tagged to Friday nights when no hand was entered. I put this to the owners. Their reply called it machine error and directed me to carry it forward. I have carried it forward every quarter since. The register page is ruled to the shifts of the quarter, and loom 19 ran one shift past the ruling, so that entry sits below the ruled lines on the register's own board cover. The cloth is still in the warehouse, dated to its Fridays. There is no line in any ledger for cloth that no hand wove.
