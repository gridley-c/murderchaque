The Eleventh Tread

File no. CHAQUE/005/2026 · Recorded 26 October 2026 · Location: Grounds of Sonning Bishop's Palace, Berkshire · Status: OPEN

Written by: Ministry of Works inspector, 1958

×

Exhibit A — retained in original condition. 24mm at f/8; captured 26 October 2026

Property notes

A medieval stone keep of rubble walling standing on open ground and recorded in monochrome. Dressed parapets with squared merlons crown the wall head. An external stair of solid masonry rises the right hand face of the keep to a dark doorway at first floor level set under a small gabled stone hood, the stair with a solid cheek wall and no handrail. A gabled wing of two storeys with mullioned and transomed windows and a pitched tiled roof abuts the left flank at a straight vertical joint, with one chimney stack behind its ridge. Windows at ground level carry leaded diamond glazing. A single modern interpretation panel on one post stands at the foot of the stair buttress. The foreground is level gravel with sparse growth at the wall foot. No persons are present.

History of the incident

In April 1957 the keep was attended by me, H. R____, inspector for the Ministry of Works, for the re-survey under the revised Town and Country Planning schedules. The stair is eleven solid masonry treads with a solid cheek wall, no rail, rising the right hand face to the hooded door. The 1913 survey counts twelve. I reconciled the file by recording that the lowest tread had been rebuilt at some date after 1913 and that one tread had gone under the gravel spread at the foot, and I entered twelve as the standing count with a note that the make-up could not be certified. My rod put eleven treads on the ground at every passage.

On 17 October 1958 I attended again for the second count before the list was re-advertised. Eleven treads. On 19 October, before the Office mason left, eleven again. Before I signed, one Fenton, foreman of the county estate masons, came to me with a requisition order book from his own service. On the third of March 1956 his yard had been ordered to cut and set one tread at the foot of the keep stair of the material his yard held for another Office contract, night work, the old tread taken away as the order permitted, the cost to the Office account. The ordering officer's signature column is struck through in red pencil, twice, and initialed by the Office hand named on the cyclostyled copy his yard kept, the Office's own copy never having left the Office. No official survey note records this work. The file I now hold records a count of twelve standing since 1913 and, in Fenton's book, the removal of one tread set at the foot on the third of March 1956.

The keep as I surveyed it stands with eleven treads. The file requires twelve. I did not count twelve because the fabric did not contain twelve, and I cannot certify the interval because the only record of the removal is a struck signature in a mason's order book where no survey should have needed one. The interpretation panel at the stair foot describes the stairs as original. The Office copy of the order was never sent. The schedule box is empty. The book is initialled by my hand at eleven, and the standing count of twelve is not mine.
