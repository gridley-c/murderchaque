The Pavilion

File no. CHAQUE/001/2026 · Recorded 18 October 2026 · Location: Pavilion ground among the pines, Telford, Surrey · Status: OPEN

Entertained by: pavilion caretaker and groundsman, 1934

Exhibit A — retained in original condition. None survived transmission.

Property notes

A single storey timber hall of rectangular plan standing on soft ground among tall pines. Walls weatherboarded and painted pale below the eaves. Roof tiled, hipped at the ends, with a central gambrel gable over the long side. The gable face is boarded in herringbone pattern between green painted battens, carries four gilt numerals reading 1883, and has a square clock face set into it above the numerals. A small window sits over the clock in the gable head. The centre of the long side stands open and dark under a plank verandah carried on posts, with bench seats at the verandah rails and panelled windows flanking the opening. The forecourt is laid to gravel with worn patches of grass, attended by a flagpole, a bench, and a litter bin. Lean to sheds and tall timber gates stand to the left of the approach, and a dark stained outbuilding carrying a bright green cabinet stands to the right, with a short white painted post guarding a young tree near the path.

History of the incident

On the morning of Saturday 12 May 1934 the caretaker A. W____ opened the pavilion for the first fixture of the season and found the gable clock stopped at eleven minutes past six. He noted it in the building diary, wound the movement, and set it by his watch. It kept time until the Tuesday. On the Wednesday it stopped again at the same reading. The maker, W. H____ of Wellington, came out on 19 May 1934, cleaned the movement, and left a signed slip in the diary: movement sound, case sound, hands true. Under it he wrote the serial and the year of first sale: 1881. The gilt numerals on the gable read 1883, and the opening of the pavilion is minuted for 5 May 1883, the gift of the clock recorded in the same sitting.

The matter would have rested as a clerk's error had the secretary, Miss R. D____, not written to the maker's firm in June 1934 to ask whether the clock had been sold twice. The firm's reply, pasted into the diary, names the purchaser of the 1881 movement as a Mrs. E. T____ of this parish, the same lady minuted in 1883 as presenting the clock at the opening. No other entry for her exists in their ledger. That autumn A. W____ took down the case and photographed the gable for the insurance valuation. The prints came back from the chemist in Wellington on 3 October 1934. They show the clock, the herringbone boarding, and the green battens, all as they stand. The space under the clock, where the numerals are fixed, is blank in every print.

The pavilion stands in the plate that accompanies this file. The numerals 1883 are on it, gilt on the green batten under the clockface, plain for any reader to verify with their own eyes. The insurance prints of 3 October 1934 say they were not there on that date. The minute of 5 May 1883 says the clock was presented at the opening; the maker's slip dates its sale two years before the building was opened. I have entered each paper under its own date, each is true by the hand that made it, and the file will not close. The diary page for 4 October 1934 is cut out flush at the stub. What A. W____ wrote of the prints is not in the record.
