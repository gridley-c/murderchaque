The Pavilion

File no. CHAQUE/001/2026 · Recorded 18 October 2026 · Location: Pavilion ground among the pines, Telford, Surrey · Status: OPEN

Entertained by: pavilion caretaker and groundsman, 1934

Exhibit A — retained in original condition. None survived transmission.

Property notes

A single storey timber hall of rectangular plan standing on soft ground among tall pines. Walls weatherboarded and painted pale below the eaves. Roof tiled, hipped at the ends, with a central gambrel gable over the long side. The gable face is boarded in herringbone pattern between green painted battens, carries four gilt numerals reading 1883, and has a square clock face set into it above the numerals. A small window sits over the clock in the gable head. The centre of the long side stands open and dark under a plank verandah carried on posts, with bench seats at the verandah rails and panelled windows flanking the opening. The forecourt is laid to gravel with worn patches of grass, attended by a flagpole, a bench, and a litter bin. Lean to sheds and tall timber gates stand to the left of the approach, and a dark stained outbuilding carrying a bright green cabinet stands to the right, with a short white painted post guarding a young tree near the path.

History of the incident

On Saturday 5 May 1883 the pavilion was opened, and every paper in this file that touches the gable says the same thing: four gilt numerals reading 1883 fixed below the clock face, there from the first. The minute of the opening records the gift of the clock that day; the maker's signed slip pasted in the building diary gives the movement's first sale as 1881, unsold stock, nothing more. In May 1934 I, A. W____, caretaker, entered the spring repairs in the diary as the job has always run them: gutters cleared, battens made good, the gilt of the date gone over where the winter had taken it. The numerals were on the gable then, as in every season's note I keep.

That autumn the clubhouse was valued for insurance and I photographed the gable for the valuation. The prints came back from the chemist in Guildford dated 3 October 1934, six exposures, sharp in every part. They show the herringbone boarding, the green battens, the square clock face, the small window over it, all as they stand. The batten under the clock, where the numerals are fixed, is blank in every print. No numerals, no drill holes, no shadow of a fixing. The valuer signed his schedule on 10 October 1934 describing the date in gilt; the prints in his own envelope say it was not there seven days before.

I wrote to the maker's firm, to the parish, and to the signwriter in Woking who regilded the name boards in 1929. No order for the numerals, no invoice, no scaffolding entry, no minute of any committee, turns up anywhere. The pavilion in the plate that accompanies this file carries the numerals, plain for any reader to verify above the verandah. I checked the fixings on 15 October 1934: old screws, paint under the heads, gilt worn as the clock's case is worn, as if they had hung fifty years. Someone put them up, by hand, on a day, for a reason, and the record holds no trace of the hands, the day, or the reason. The diary page for 4 October 1934, where I wrote what the prints showed, is cut out flush at the stub. Every other paper says the numerals were always there. They were not there on 3 October 1934, and I cannot find when they came.
